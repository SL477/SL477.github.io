This is at least the fourth time I have re-watched this series.
But this time I had a lot less time for the terrible parts of this series.
Doctor Who's greatest strength is its variability, but it is also it's greatest weakness.
You have the wonderful Dark Water/Death in Heaven and then you have Kill the Moon, representing all that went horrifyingly wrong.

## Deep Breath

> Am I a good person? - <cite>the Doctor</cite>

Let's start here, with that quote (which I'm not sure is actually in this episode, but he says similar things through the series).
As Official Nerd Cubed pointed out in a video of his I watched, it is a completely stupid line.
In the prior episode The Time of the Doctor Matt Smith's Doctor spent 300-800 years protecting the people of Trenzalore from the Cybermen/Daleks/everyone and was prepared to keep on doing that until dying of old age or being killed by the Daleks.
The Doctor does make some callous decisions throughout the series, but all for the greater good.

We revisit the terrific trio of Madame Vastra, Jenny and Strax in Victorian London as they fight the good fight.
We see the sister ship of the Madame De Pompadour and its crew of clockwork robots butchering people (best to make strictly vegetarian robots).
And our first glimpse of Missy at the end and a mention of the Promised Land.

I'm not sure I completely appreciated the call from Matt Smith's Doctor to Clara telling her to stay with Peter Capaldi's Doctor, but it did at least explain why she stayed with him.
As the dynamic between them changed dramatically, the previous flirty relationship wasn't going to work and it did take a while for the writers to sort things out[^1].

There is a big tonal shift from the Matt Smith family drama to the Peter Capaldi dark drama.

## Into the Dalek

This was mostly stolen from Honey I Shrunk the Kids.
It didn't really add much to the concept other than going into a Dalek rather than a human.
As we saw shrinking people with the Tesseracter in Let's Kill Hitler and The Wedding of River Song, in a more original way, the episode's central concept fell flat.

It does setup Clara and Danny's romance.

## Robot of Sherwood

A good Robin Hood pantomime with Doctor Who.
We see Ben Miller as the evil Sheriff of Nottingham.
Plus there is archery, robots and a spaceship.

## Listen

The best episode of the series and one of the best Doctor Who episodes.

We see the possible great-grandchild of Clara and Danny, in Colonel Awesome Pink.
Which is a bit awkward in the middle of their first date.

The monsters may or may not exist, it is something of an open question.
We are given a glimpse into the Doctor's childhood, when the TARDIS lands on Gallifrey.

## Time Heist

A slightly different take on the heist movie, but without all of the annoying twists seen in a proper heist movie (Rick and Morty had another great take on those).

## The Caretaker

The Doctor is introduced to Danny.
I couldn't force myself to watch that again.

## Kill the Moon

If I couldn't bring myself to rewatch Caretaker then I really couldn't watch this rubbish again.
Still more memorable than any Jodie Whittaker episode.

## Mummy on the Orient Express

I could only bring myself to see this one again because of the train.
It was an ok episode.

## Flatline 

Aliens in 2D.
We see Doctor Clara running around while the Doctor is trapped in the TARDIS.

## In the Forest of the Night

Not quite as terrible as Kill the Moon, but quality control obviously got brutally murdered.

## Dark Water

Danny Pink is dead.
Clara's occasional personality trait of being a control freak is brought up again.
The Promised Land is revealed.
Missy is revealed to be the Master.

## Death in Heaven

We see a lot more of UNIT in this episode.
The Doctor's friends get a bit killed by Missy.

We see Clara's ability to lie again, as she once again becomes the Doctor.
Her occasional personality trait of being a control freak is brought up again, which is a bit rich given that she has been the number two for the much more domineering Doctor for the last couple of years and he's pulled seniority on her a few times in the series.

We see the much more tragic second death of Danny Pink and the Doctor and Clara having their sad splitting up.

[^1]: a fair few regenerations see the companions going too, and I can see why.
