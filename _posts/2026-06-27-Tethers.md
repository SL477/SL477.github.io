Stanley Jenson forced himself to walk into the terminal.
His instincts were screaming at him to run.
His bag was suspiciously full of cash from the Pan-Galactic Casino.
All the rest of the gang were either dead, captured or had fled.
He walked up to the ticket machine, he felt that talking to an actual person would give him away.

"ID please," the machine asked.
Stanley waved the eye wateringly expensive forgery at it and the machine accepted it (it was a genuine forgery from a corrupt official at the Inspectorate, one of what in the end had been three documents for those who had made it out of the vault, two of which would likely never be used).
"Destination?"

"Wherever the next passenger ship I can get to from here is going."
Stanley replied.
He fed a copious number of notes into the machine and took the virtual ticket.

He all but ran into the next capsule.
His fellow passengers looked at him oddly, his sweat stained shirt with a hint of blood on it and beaten looking rucksack.
The two of them were impeccably dressed and prepared for an eight hour journey up the space elevator, with books and food.
Either they hadn't wanted to go into temporal stasis for the trip, or like Stanley hadn't prepared for that part.
After what felt like an eternity the doors closed behind him and the capsule was shunted and pulled around onto the tether of the space elevator.

The hundred thousand kilometres of tether contained countless thousands of capsules heading up to space and a second tether for the return journey to Earth.
Mount Chimborazo was the location of the second busiest space elevator.
The first one at Lake Victoria in Africa and the other, now fallen elevator, on the island of Kalimantan.
Given how ridiculously expensive space elevators were and the relative impoverishment of Earth, it was unlikely that the Kalimantan elevator would be replaced anytime soon.

Stanley sat on one of the many seats where he could watch out of the window.
His two companions also watching the view.
A small river of containers on trains flowed up and down the mountain.
The Earth bound cargo was off to various destinations in South America and to ocean based ports.
Those going up first went to Eagle Station, the giant ring station most of the way up the tether (none went to the large asteroid sitting as the counterweight at the very end of the tether).
After Eagle Station the containers could flow to Luna, or to the Jovian Alliance or various other colonies around the Sol System.
Some found there way to the starships and off to the various other territories in the Federal Alliance and beyond.

The domed farmland and cities around mountain, and the rest of the mountain range could be seen.
The dull grey light of the Sun hiding many of the colours.
Then the capsule reached the lower edge of cloud cover.
The fallout clouds had lessened over the decades since the dramatic conclusion of the Conclave War, and apparently would one day be a thing of the past.
Earth's new impoverishment was partly due to a curtailment of its exports of Terran plants and animals, due to the effects of the nuclear winter.
Fortunately, or unfortunately depending on your perspective, the gene vaults on Luna had taken the slack and now Selene was reaping the benefits.

Stanley explored the pod a bit while the capsule climbed through the fallout cloud.
There was not much more than a toilet, a water dispenser and a variety of seats.
His two companions had set themselves up in the corner and did not seem very approachable.

After half an hour the capsule made it above the clouds.
There were many patches where the distant ground and ocean could be seen.
As the capsule rose higher and higher the curvature of the Earth became more apparent.
Only a few hundred metres away a downward going pod went past.
In the further distance aircraft could occasionally be seen.
On most places on Earth there tended to be more spacecraft than aircraft, but the space elevator meant that most people nearby tended to use that (some people really didn't want to spend eight hours in an elevator and Stanley was certainly getting sympathetic to that point of view).

Without much fanfare the sun set.
Stanley tried to sleep to pass the time.
Luna, Earth's natural moon, was now fully prominent in the sky.
The great band of solar collectors across its equator made it appear as if someone had cut the moon in half (they were almost eternally cleaned and repaired by an army of robots, which had spurred on the establishment of a series of titanic solar sails.
One of these could comfortably be used to wrap the moon).
Straight above and very far away was the large ring of Eagle Station.

Stanley was trying to ignore the periodic flashes of light, these were not from cosmic rays as he was too close to the Earth for that, but from one of the planet's warp lanes.
Ships appeared and disappeared on their way to other star systems (the minimum safe distance of a jump was around half a light year.
Attempting less generally saw your ship arriving as a perfectly flat disk before explosively trying to become ship shaped again).
They was a flash of light as a ship full of lamb sent up from the space elevator ahead of Stanley went to Epsilon Eridani.
Then a slightly more muted flash as a starship from Epsilon Eridani arrived full of lamb to deliver to South America.
Stanley could only just see the starships the size of mountains at the warp lane as they fired their engines, most of the warp lanes terminated at roughly the distance to the moon.

Stanley woke up, Eagle Station was now an enormous presence above him.
The space elevator terminated at the hub (or at least its capsules did).
The huge wheel was spun for gravity and large cargo shuttles were endlessly docking to its outside or being flung off into space.
Millions could and did live on the habitable inner surface, huge farms had been planted on its spare space to help feed the population both here and on Earth.

He was now practically weightless, Eagle Station sat in geostationary orbit above the Earth.

"All passengers to fasten seatbelts and prepare for cable detach."
Came the voice over the PA.
Stanley floated over to his seat and attached himself.
His two companions much more adept, had been swooping around the cabin and they also tethered themselves to their seats.

A big mechanical arrangement plucked the pod off of the cable at the hub of the station and then gave it a shove so that it drifted straight into one of the spokes down to the rim of the wheel.
On the other cable a similar mechanism grabbed pods out of another spoke and attached it to the descending cable for it to start the return journey.
Stanley tried not to flinch and act cool like his companions when the capsule was suddenly flung through space and into one of the spokes.
It didn't help that it was now under power and heading what would be downwards, but the convoluted flip and zero gravity had destroyed his internal reference frame.
Gravity rapidly reasserted itself and Stanley's stomach lurched as it worked out which way gravity was now.

The capsule doors opened.
Nervously Stanley edged out, he was relieved by the lack of security forces mobbing him the moment he walked out.
He hurried through the terminus of the space elevator, following the instructions in his head, aiming for the train under the rim.
He did not have time to stop for food or water, and he was still worried that the police were going to stop him.
More money went on his train ticket.

The train offered another view of space as it ran around the underside of the space station.
It arrived near Stanley's assigned shuttle and he rushed out.
He got a bit of a view of the giant wheat fields on the opposite side of the station, but he quickly made it to the space port.
The inspector gave Stanley a quizzical look as he inspected his ticket and passport.
But he stamped the passport and sent Stanley on his way.
As the shuttle fell away from Eagle Station, Stanley finally relaxed.
When the starship engaged its warp drive and jumped out of the Sol System, Stanley raised a glass to celebrate his freedom.
