After making the mistake of watching Star Wars Episode 9 I had avoided Star Wars since (more or less).
But I decided to celebrate Star Wars Day a bit late.

Episode 2 suffers a bit from the terrible love story/horror movie of Anakin and Padmé, but once you get over that you get to see a lot more of civilian life in the Star Wars galaxy.

The speeder chase at the beginning rather demonstrates why we probably won't be seeing flying cars anytime soon.
Zam Wessel's speeder crashing to the ground shows what would happen in a flying car crash (gravity making most accidents a fatal one).

I'm not sure about the cinematic choice of having large amounts of action on Geonosis and Tatoine.
Both desert worlds look very similar.
I also don't like and never really have liked the Star Wars trope of having the big battle somewhere conveniently away from large numbers of civilians[^1].
I mean I get it, they want a low rating so that kids can watch it, but the days when armies politely arranged a time and place for battle, then stopped for a lunch break, was a long time ago.

Given the fact that the factory was largely automated, the big secret desert base isn't too unrealistic in the Star Wars universe.
But given that the Separatists have thousands of star systems, they could just use the existing infrastructure on one or more of them to build their droid army.
We get enough shots of how massive Coresant is, it would have been easier for the Trade Federation to build their battle droids there (or to make it less obvious their components) and simply hid in the masses of traffic in the city[^2].

The space battle over Geonosis has to be the best one in any Star Wars film.
Possibly in any film.
It's likely not realistic by any stretch of the imagination, but it's epic.

The tactics of the clone wars leave much to be desired.
Until about the time of the machine gun men basically stood around in formation firing and being fired upon.
When your adversary was armed with flintlock muskets and needed a good few minutes to reload, then your chances of being hit were a lot less.
The first few minutes of world war 1[^3] showed what happened when you marched in formation straight at an enemy machine gun nest.
It became fashionable to put a trench between yourself and your enemies' bullets.
In World War 2 films, trenches were out of fashion due to tanks and bombers, but generally you tried to put a building or a tank between yourself and your enemy; or if you didn't have one of those things you at least make yourself a smaller target.

[^1]: Ok so on Geonosis there were some civilians, but within about 10 seconds of the jedi arriving they had all fled and conveniently all of the military infrastructure was easily distinguishable from civilian.

[^2]: long term it wouldn't work, because the moment the clone wars started the factories would be the wrong side of the front lines. But the Star Wars universe isn't short of planetwide cities.

[^3]: and earlier the American Civil War
