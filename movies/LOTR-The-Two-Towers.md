Not quite as good as the first film, but I think better than the next one despite suffering a bit from middle trilogy syndrome, is the second LOTR film.

Frodo and Sam meander around and don't exactly follow what they did in the book (though I'm not sure they had a great deal to do).

Merry and Pippin are carried along by orcs and then the ents.

The three hunters run across Rohan and then ride back.
Then they have a minor skirmish with warg riders, rather than the full pitched battle in the fall of the Westfold and retreat to Helm's Deep.
Which I'm guessing was to save a lot of money and make the battle of Helm's Deep more epic (otherwise there would have been the fight at the River Isen and a series of stands as they get pushed back to Helm's Deep).
It has the unfortunate consequence of making it look like Rohan is just Edoras and a convenient castle in the middle of nowhere.
Which continues my theme of what I don't like about the films!

The battle of Helm's Deep is certainly epic, for some reason the elves of Lothlorian decide to go there rather than fight alongside the elves of Mirkwood to destroy Dol Guldor[^1].
We are treated to a giant bomb made by Saruman and ten thousand orcs.
It shows the huge advantage of defence, as a much smaller army holds the castle.

The ents' battle against Isenguard is suitably epic, if dramatically one sided.
In the films Gandalf doesn't race from the ents' battle at Isenguard to collect Huorns from Treebeard (he just magically knows to send them).
But instead goes to collect Eomer, who in the films is banished and in the books is busy fighting the Orcs the whole time.
Although I can't argue that the charge of the Rohirrim is nice and epic, although in reality gravity likely killed them all.

There's a lot more use of Osgiliath in the films, with Faramir having the conversation he has with Frodo in the Forbidden Pool there instead.
I suppose that there is only so much walking that we can watch Frodo do.
Frodo in turn wonders what Gandalf's plan was to get into Mordor, whether it was to chance the Black Gate, go through Cirith Ungol or go right around the mountains around Mordor.

[^1]: Which is what they do in the books, as the War of the Ring is bigger there. The Dwarves of the Lonely Mountain face an army of Orcs who went north from Mordor to kill everyone from the Hobbit book.
