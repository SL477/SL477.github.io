1. Trace the water you drink from precipitation to tap.

I have been to Bewl Water the local reservoir.
My mum and I cycled around it, a massive mistake.
We thought it would take a short amount of time, but my sister learnt to ride a bike while we were gone.

2. How many days till the moon is full? (Slack of two days allowed.)

I think it was full a couple of days ago.
So about 26.

3. What soil series are you standing on?

Earth like soil.

4. What was the total rainfall in your area last year (July–June)? (Slack: 1″ for every 20″.)

0-20,000,000".
To paraphrase XKCD "any number you can fit on a whiteboard basically rounds down to zero".
Admittedly it was talking about astronomy, but I'm sure it's a general mathematical rule.
   
5. When was the last time a fire burned in your area?

Probably when they did fireworks at the beginning of October for reasons best known to themselves.

6. What were the primary subsistence techniques of the culture that lived in your area before you?

As a descendant of the culture that lived in my area (well more or less).
And being 100 metres from a farm.
I'm going to make the wild stab in the dark that it was farming.

7. Name five native edible plants in your region and their season(s) of availability.

- Wheat, October or so. Not quite sure if you can count said farm's crops as native.
- Corn. October.

8. From what direction do winter storms generally come in your region?

West from the Atlantic coast.

9. Where does your garbage go?

It used to go to Sevenoaks rubbish dump/quarry near Otford.
Now to the recycling depot by the M25 junction and the place near the old gas cylinder.

10. How long is the growing season where you live?

March to October.

11. On what day of the year are the shadows the shortest where you live?

Probably the summer Solace.
But I don't have the faintest idea.

12. When do the deer rut in your region, and when are the young born?

The rutting season is when we aren't allowed in Knole Park.
And some time after that.
There was a doe and her fawn near me, I first saw them in the field by the station and later in the nearby woods.

13. Name five grasses in your area. Are any of them native?

Wheat. The green grass. The all but dead grass. The grass I got from Tesco's (other grass is available).

14. Name five resident and five migratory birds in your area.

Residents: Kites, herons, crows, swans, magpies (I think one of those nests on the roof, and I think it's chick has moved out now).
I do live right by a farm and wild fowl reserve (they want to build all over said farm).
The hawk/bigger bird of prey on the top of the nearby hill.
The robin in next door's tree and occasionally my garden.
The many varieties of ducks in the lakes.

Migratory: Canadian Geese, sea gulls (they come a long way inland every now and again), parakeets (not exactly native and I don't think that they migrate, but they look neat), the brown ones that like to wash themselves in the pond, some of the thousands of crows that flew away earlier in the year (there are still thousands of crows nearby), the ducks in the lakes that are migratory.
My lack of knowledge of which birds migrate and which don't is rather hindering me on this.
Maybe next time I walk by the wild fowl reserve I should stop looking at all of the horses and sheep.
But I feel that if I wanted to take up bird watching I wouldn't need to go very far.

15. What is the land use history of where you live?

Farming, market town and quarry.
Military base.
Grace and favour country house of the Foreign Secretary (and used as the US embassy in the awful programme the diplomat).

If you go a bit further afield to next town or more, then High Rocks near Tunbridge Wells was used for filming Rings of Power.

16. What primary ecological event/process influenced the land form where you live? (Bonus special: what’s the evidence?)

Glacial valleys. Evidence being the large valleys which used to hold glaciers.
The river, evidence being the nearby river (admittedly more of a stream).

And chalk mining if you want to count that, the evidence being the large exposed chalk cliff and all of the lakes from other quarries.

17. What species have become extinct in your area?

All nearby mice when my old cat was alive (R.I.P. Tigger).
The lack of mammoths, sabertooth tigers, dinosaurs, wild boar, means probably loads.
If the developers have their way, then the sheep two fields away will be.

18. What are the major plant associations in your region?

Ones that like chalk, rivers, the North Downs, ancient woodlands, farms, etc.

19. From where you’re reading this, point north.

It's to my left.

20. What spring wildflower is consistently among the first to bloom where you live?

No idea.
Hopefully the lavender in the garden will have a go.
