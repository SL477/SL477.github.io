> I was there. I was there the day that Horus slew the Emperor. <cite>Garviel Loken - Horus Rising/The End and the Death</cite>

I have started re-reading the Horus Heresy and hopefully reading many of the books I jumped on the way.

The series is a bit odd, the first few entries have Horus's lightning fall to Chaos, Heresy and treachery.
Then post Istvan it really slows down.
Originally the Black Library envisioned a ten book series to capture the main events of the Heresy and then for it to be over and done with quickly[^2].
I think it was about ten years ago that I started reading the Horus Heresy books and twenty years since it was published and only this year did the last book come out.
There were 54 main books, 18 Primarch books and 10 Siege of Tera books[^1].

The main reason it came about was that Games Workshop's management did not want the Warhammer 40k universe to progress.
It spent years on 999 M41, the poor Ultramarines charging all over the galaxy in one year fighting all of the fires.
There were only so many books they could sell on battles were it didn't matter who won because they were never going to advance the timeline enough for it to matter who took Unimportantis 9 and it's shipyards so vital that they have never been mentioned before[^3]. 

The Heresy series allowed the authors to finally get a galaxy wide crisis and perspective.
With the xenos largely out of the picture thanks to the Great Crusade, you didn't have the problem that the later Dawn of Fire series faced where you flicked from one enemy to another and it made it appear that the Indomitus Crusade appeared to achieve nothing[^4].

## Original plan

At the beginning of the Heresy Horus wipes out a third of the Sons of Horus, Emperor's Children and World Eaters (the loyalists).
But is delayed by Angron deploying to the surface and a loyalist ship escaping into the warp and revealing what is happening.

If Horus's plan at Istvan 3 had gone off without a hitch, then he would have had the better part of three legions obviously on his side.
He also had the Night Lords, Iron Warriors, Alpha Legion, Word Bearers and Death Guard in reserve.

Against him were the loyalist space marine legions, who Horus would still be commander of.
The Ultramarines were awaiting the Word Bearers at Calth, nicely out of the way of the path to Terra.
The Space Wolves had been sent to 'deal with' the Thousand Sons, in orders that Horus had interfered with to provoke a blood bath and this had drawn the Silent Sisterhood and Legio Sinistra to Prospero from Terra.
The Blood Angels had been sent to Signus Prime, a nice out the way bloodbath full of daemons.
The Dark Angels had also been sent on a wild goose chase.
Jagharti Khan and the White Scars had been bottled up in Chondax by the Alpha Legion, on the orders of Horus at Ullenor so prior to his official fall.
The Imperial Fists had been taken by The Emperor to Terra to fortify the Imperial Palace and Horus can't overrule his boss.

The Raven Guard, Iron Hands and Salamanders were the victims of the Drop Site Massacre.
Ferrus Mannus of the Iron Hands was assumed would be turned by Fulgrim of the Emperor's Children[^5].
The Raven Guard's Horus sympathisers had been sent to their deaths roughly on Horus's orders (not exactly his intention).
But the Raven Guard were basically famed for small special forces missions rather than big battles.
The Salamanders were also a fairly small legion, Vulkan as something of a humanitarian[^6], he wouldn't side with Chaos maniac Horus, but might side with the more reasonable Horus.

Horus's signature/only strategy is the speartip thrust to the throat.
An overwhelming strike to take out the leadership of a planet/nation/etc., so it can be inferred that he would immediately head to Terra to take out The Emperor, Council of Terra and the Administratum.
Istvan was a fairly long way from Terra, so someone would likely notice well before his 3-8 legions made it to Terra.
So effectively four legions against eight, so a two to one advantage in numbers of Space Marines.
Horus generally appeared to scorn the contributions of the human troops, so probably wouldn't be that bothered by the fact that his human auxiliary troops in his armada would be outnumbered by the troops at Terra and its nearby systems.

With Mars on his side he could boost his numbers from theirs.
And the Loyalists might not be in a position to blockade Mars if Horus fleet appeared shortly after Mars's declaration of rebellion[^7].

The Emporer is seen as Horus main opponent.
As the most powerful Psyker ever it is seen that he could just snap his fingers and wipe out the traitors in one go[^8].
He obviously can't, as otherwise he wouldn't need the legions in the first place.
Psychic powers, at least in the books, are left a bit vague and at the Emporer's scale aren't on the tabletop or rulebook.
So it may well be that Horus intended to defeat the Emporer's armies and then just accept the Emporer's surrender.

The Emporer is key to the Astronomican and to soul bind astropaths to communicate through the warp, so both FTL travel and communications[^9].
So killing him out of hand would be inadvisable.
But it is possible that Horus intended for Him[^10] to have an enforced retirement.

### What was his plan post rebellion?

Without all of the Chaos powers and mass human sacrifice (although probably with the occasional mass genocide to keep his hand in); Horus probably wouldn't exterminate humanity in a fit of remorse[^11].

He likely intended to take up the mantle of Emperor of Mankind and rule in a military dictatorship, like The Emperor had done prior to the Ullenor triumph.
The seven traitor Primarchs who stood with him would most likely be handsomely rewarded and sent back out to stamp out the remaining loyalists and then continue the Great Crusade.

He would likely welcome the other Primarchs who went to his side.
He brought Magnus and the Thousand Sons in, we don't quite know when, but they had been expelled by the Loyalists in the Burning of Prospero and joined the Siege of Terra on the Traitors' side.

Robute Guilliman and the Ultramarines I can see making a Theoretical/Practical decision and most likely agreeing that the 500 Worlds of Ultramar become some sort of allies/protectorate of Horus Imperium.
Without full Chaos Horus and the Mark of Calth, joining a victorious Horus might be more politically acceptable.
Especially if The Emperor (former) is still alive and well.

The Loyalty is it's own reward gang.
Dorn (dead with the Imperial Fists at Terra), Lion El'Johnson (Dark Angels), Ferrus Mannus (Iron Hands) and Leman Russ (Space Wolves) would all need dealing with at some point or another.

Vulkan and the Salamanders would likely side with Horus rather than have Nocturne destroyed.

Sanguineous and the Blood Angels, they are part of the Loyalty is it's own reward gang but are much too important to be lumped in with the Iron Hands.
They are fighting to the last and Glorious Hawk Boy is going to be inciting a full scale Loyalist counter rebellion against Horus[^12].

Lion El'Johnson, despite previously being mentioned in the Loyalty is it's own reward gang, is a curious case.
Half of his legion rebelled After the Heresy.
Many of the rebels thought that they were fighting on the Loyalist side and that Lionel was the traitor.
Secrets and lies are something of a currency in the Dark Angels legion, and many suspected that Lionel didn't reappear for the siege as he was waiting to see who would win[^13].
So it is possible that he would join Horus post Heresy if he was victorious.

Corvus Corax, I know little about the Raven Guard and had to Google his name, so obviously he was a highly memorable character.
His thing was that he hated dictators and slavers (like Angron), but still sided with The Emperor, Terra's biggest dictator and his fleet crewed with slaves.
So obviously principles meant a lot to him.
Without all of the baggage of having his troops massacred on Istvan 5 and being hunted for a couple of months, surrender to Horus is likely more acceptable than off hand extermination.

## Dark mirrors

Many of the loyalist and traitor legions are seen as dark mirrors of each other.
With the prime candidate of this trope being the Imperial Fists and Iron Warriors.
The fortress builders and fortress besiegers.
But then things start getting complicated as both the Dark Angels and Raven Guard appear to be mirrored by the Alpha Legion.

### Ultramarines

Their thing is empire building.
Guilliman's aim is to leave each world he conquers better than it was and many go into the 500 worlds.

The Word Bearers do something similar, they religiously indoctrinate their conquered worlds into their cult of the Emperor.
Then after Monarchia into Chaos. Monarchia could be a warning to them as well.

Horus and Guilliman are also very alike.
They both have a burning ambition in them.
For Horus Lupercal the ambition is personal, he should be Emporer and saviour of the galaxy.
The Luna Wolves/Sons of Horus their war cry is Lupercal.
Guilliman's ambition is for Ultramar (and secondarily the Imperium), with recognition for him a not unwelcome side effect.
The Ultramarines cry is "we march for Macrage".

We see the differences between the two legions.
In False Gods Horus is struck down by the Anetheme and the Sons of Horus go to pieces, the universe with no Horus is unthinkable.
In the Battle of the Abyss, Guilliman is believed lost, but the Ultramarines are able to carry on with the battle.
Similarly in The Unremembered Empire Guilliman is once again presumed dead and the Ultramarines stoically get one with everything.
Also in The End and the Death Horus is actually killed and the Sons of Horus flee the siege of Terra.

Both legions have a measure of training for life after combat.
Loken has lessons with Siderman and the Ultramarines are all being coached to rule over Ultramar (with the tetrarchs running large parts of it).

### Iron Warriors and the Imperial Fists

Both legions specialise in building things and sieges (both sides of them).
But there is a fair bit of nuance to that.

The Iron Warriors will take enormous casualties in grinding attritional warfare (like the Death Guard).

While the Imperial Fists will try to avoid that and win through doing something more clever.
They also specialise in more mobile warfare like the Iron Hands.

### Alpha Legion, Raven Guard and Dark Angels

These are the three special forces legions.

In my mind at least, the Dark Angels are more like the Marine Corps/Mountaineer Regiment of one of our regular militaries.
More specialist than a traditional army Infantryman, but not proper special forces.
They also bear similarities to the White Scars in their various sub-divisions (arguably as the First Legion they had to specialise in everything, so arguments for any other legion).

The Raven Guard are more the SAS/Delta Force/etc. a small gang of elite highly trained warriors rather than a mass force.
The Space Wolves similarly often send out Wolf packs as special forces teams.

The Alpha Legion are more like the CIA/MI6.
They often recruit human agents to do their dirty work and they are treated as almost full legionaries.
No one knows where the Alpha Legion properly stand.
A hard contrast to Alpharius is Rogul Dorn of the Imperial Fists, who only properly discovers lying in Saturnine[^14].

### White Scars, Space Wolves and Thousand Sons

Another triple!
The White Scars and Space Wolves both have a sort of tribal traditional way of sipping from the warp.
In complete contrast to the Thousand Sons who are full on diving head first into the warp and bargaining with daemons.

The two moderate legions argue for limits on warp research and the librarius at Nicear.
While the Alpha Legion argue for no limits and anything is allowed.
This worked out well in the Burning of Prospero...

There isn't really another legion that the Thousand Sons overlap with.
So maybe one of the purged legions was massively psychic.

A good contrast between the Primarchs are Leman Russ of the Space Wolves and Lion El'Johnson of the Dark Angels.
A noble man pretending to be a savage and a savage pretending to be a noble man[^15].

### Blood Angels and World Eaters

The two angry legions.
The Blood Angels try to be better than their cursed desire for blood and with the coming of Sanguinus became more noble.
While the World Eaters were cursed by their idiot Primarch[^16].

The Blood Angels are basically the best legion who everyone else aspires to be.
Horus thinks that Sanguinus should have been warmaster and Guilliman crowns him Emporer of Imperium Secundus.
