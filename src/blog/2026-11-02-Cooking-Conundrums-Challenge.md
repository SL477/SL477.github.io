1. Do you enjoy cooking?

Yes.
I don't cook that much per week, as generally I cook a big batch of stuff and freeze it.
At the moment I am in a bit of a lull while catching up on the eating side of the equation.

2. If you learned, who taught you to cook?

My mum.
She cooks a lot more than I do.
Plus a few cooking shows.

3. Do you follow cooking bloggers? Who are your favorites?

No.

4. Do you own any cookbooks? If so, which do you refer to most often?

James May's Oh Cook is the one I use most often at my place.
Pinch of Nom and Delia Smith's cook book are ones that I use a lot at my parents' house.
At some point I need to look through my Jamie Oliver cookbook and the Student's Cookbook.

5. Do you like to watch cooking shows? If so, which ones?

The Great British Bake Off is my favourite one.
After the technical challenge on Cream Horns the other year I had a go myself and they weren't that bad.
That and a yule log are the only things I have actually tried from the show.
A problem I've found is that either they may things by hand that are complicated for a person to do but are effectively solved by the supermarket[^1].
Or they make the most giant and convoluted desert you've ever seen[^2].

James May's Oh Cook was a brilliant show.
It was effectively his introduction to cooking (he had cooked before) and as such didn't suppose too much prior knowledge.
Plus they showed off a few nifty gadgets to speed things up. From that and his book I made a few things.

Jimmy's Food Factory.
Not exactly a cooking show, although you would struggle to classify it as anything else.
He built a few weird and wonderful contraptions that imitated various devices that factories have to made food.
E.g a cannon like device to make a prawn cracker disc, that just has to be dropped into the deep fat fryer to become a prawn cracker.
And the dismantled wardrobe for making concentrated apple juice.

6. Which cuisine have you cooked the most often? Which have you never attempted but would like to?

If we aren't talking British food on the grounds that I'm British and hence have cooked that more than every other cuisine put together.
Then I would say Tex-Mex.
I effectively discovered making it through James May's book and a few different kits in Tesco's/Lidl.
Chilli Con Carne is the best, though fajitas are also brilliant.

An honourable mention to Indian/Thai food.
I have made a fair few curries, but mostly from kits or cooking sauces.

I would like to attempt Sri Lankan food at some point.
The long closed Kottu Kottu did a brilliant mutton Kottu rotti dish[^3].

My attempt at an Indonesian curry hit so many substitutions that it was basically a chicken madras with extra steps.

7. What time of day or day of the week do you prefer to cook?

I make the roast on Sunday morning.
So likely Saturday or Monday for additional cooking.

8. What’s the condiment or ingredient you use the most often?

Before I moved Peri Peri salt (I need to get some more).
It certainly makes salt more interesting.

Generally tomato ketchup as my go to condiment.

Garlic appears to be in absolutely everything and earlier I ended up freezing the remains of my garlic.

9. Do you meal plan? If so, how far in advance? If not, would you like to?

I have sometimes been amazingly efficient and bought stuff for multiple recipes at once and planned everything.
But generally I just have a load of pre prepared stuff in the freezer that I've done or bought.

I did subscribe to Hello Fresh for a while.
Oh the incredible quantities of packaging.
The stress as it all slowly deteriorates.
The knowledge that another box of randomness was on its way.
But I gained a load of recipes and if anyone is moving house, then their boxes are very high quality.

10. Do you prefer to cook from a recipe or make it up as you go along?

Recipe. I can give the impression that I'm making the roast up as I go along, but that's just because I've got all of the timings memorised from doing it so often.
I'm not afraid to completely disagree with your ingredients, timings and methods.
But at the end of the day that Chicken Madras was mostly cooked to my Indonesian Chicken curry recipe (from Hello Fresh), even if the only ingredient it had in common was the chicken.
