To summarise, it's just a tool.

While I will occasionally use it's input on the code of this website, it is not going anywhere near the content.

Also I'm not paying a subscription for an AI.
So the AI is going to be the free one in Ollama, LLMsForAll and free tiers[^1].

The first AI model I ever trained was one of the chat bots that you give the script of the Bee Movie or Shakespeare too and get hilarious answers to questions from in return.
And that's pretty much what LLMs are, just with a lot more text.

I do have something of an objection to the large scale plagiarism used to gather the training materials for the models.

But another objection is that as they just put words that sound convincing together, they can be very good at creating a load of gibberish (which AI companies like to call hallucinations, so that they can pretend that LLMs are sentient).

The other problem is the verification problem.
You don't know who the AI is copying when it gives you an answer.
Whether it's from someone who knows what they are talking about, someone who made it up or the LLM just put a stream of convincing nonsense together.
So you end up checking the AI's homework, so you may as well have done it yourself.

Token billing is an interesting concept!
You can tell how many tokens what you are sending the AI is (1 token= 4 characters).
But how many the AI will use to respond, talk to itself, etc. is all unknown when you click send.
As there is a degree of randomness in the models it wouldn't necessarily use the same number twice.
And you have to trust the company on the other end.

It would be nice if an AI could be made that wasn't an LLM so that it could actually think.
As I do like the Minds in the Culture.

[^1]: At work I get to play with Gemini.
